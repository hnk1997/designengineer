The Power of Empty States: Why the First Blank Screen Matters

When we think about product design, we often focus on the big things like the homepage, the main flow, or the dashboard full of data. But what about the very first moment when there’s nothing?

That blank screen is called an empty state. And it might just be one of the most overlooked opportunities in product design.

The Problem With Ignoring Empty States

Too often, products treat empty states as placeholders. A simple “No data yet” or “Start by adding X.” That’s it. The assumption is that users will already know what to do next.

But here’s the catch: users rarely know. An uninspired empty state can make your product feel unfinished, confusing, or even unhelpful. In early moments, this can create frustration and drive people to drop off before they’ve even experienced value.

Why Empty States Matter

Think about it. When users first open your app, they haven’t generated data yet. That means their very first impression is the empty state.

That first impression does three powerful things:

  1. It sets expectations about the product’s value
  2. It guides the user toward their first meaningful action
  3. It either builds momentum or kills it

In other words, the empty state isn’t an afterthought. It’s the starting line.

Designing for Momentum

Great empty states do more than fill space. They create momentum. Here’s how:

  1. Educate with purpose — Show users what the space will look like once it’s active. Give them a glimpse of the value ahead.
  2. Guide the first action — Add a clear next step, whether that’s uploading data, creating a project, or following someone.
  3. Encourage emotionally — Use tone, visuals, and microcopy to reassure users that they’re on the right track.

One of my favorite approaches is to design empty states almost like mini onboarding moments. They’re lightweight, but they pack impact.

An Example in Action

In one project, a dashboard looked polished with sample data during design reviews. But in reality, new users saw nothing but a grey screen and a line of text that read “No reports yet.”

When we redesigned it to show:

  • a friendly illustration
  • a short description of what reports could do
  • a big “Create your first report” button

engagement jumped significantly. Users weren’t stuck wondering, “Now what?” They had a clear path forward.

The Takeaway

Empty states are not gaps. They’re opportunities. Every blank screen is a chance to set the tone, guide behavior, and deliver early wins.

The next time you’re designing a flow, ask yourself: what does the user see before the data exists?

If you can design that moment with care, you’ll turn nothing into momentum.

Quick answers

What is an empty state?

The screen people see before there’s any data. In a new product, it’s usually the first impression.

Why do empty states matter?

They set expectations about the product’s value, guide the first meaningful action, and either build momentum or kill it.

What makes a good empty state?

It shows what the space will look like once it’s active, gives a clear next step and reassures people through tone and microcopy. Think of it as a mini onboarding moment.
